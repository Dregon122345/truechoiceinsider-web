3. ⚠️ The Humidity Trap: Will It Work In Your State?

The sales material advertises the potential to produce "up to 40 gallons a day." This is the maximum theoretical output under ideal conditions. We need to talk about the physics of relative humidity.

You cannot pull water from air that contains no water. If you live in Florida, Louisiana, or Hawaii (where humidity regularly sits at 70% to 90%), this system will pull gallons of water rapidly. The air is thick with moisture.

The Arid Climate Warning

If you live in a dry desert environment like Nevada, Arizona, or parts of Utah where humidity drops below 30%, this build will struggle. The machine will use electricity to cool the coils, but it will yield very little water because the ambient air is bone dry. Do not build this system if you live in a permanent desert.

4. 🚨 Critical Warning: Water Safety & Filtration

One of the biggest mistakes DIYers make with atmospheric water generation is drinking the water straight from the condensation coils. You must not do this.

When water condenses from the air, it is essentially distilled water. However, the air carries dust, airborne bacteria, and mold spores. When that moisture hits the coils, it washes those contaminants into your collection bin. Furthermore, distilled water lacks essential dietary minerals (like calcium and magnesium).

The Smart Water Box guide covers this, but we want to reiterate it: You must route the collected water through a basic carbon/micron filtration system (like a Berkey or standard inline filter) before drinking it. Treat it exactly as you would treat collected rainwater.
